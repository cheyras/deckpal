## 2026-09-26 — Scanner voice commands: a closed grammar, pending chips, and an opt-in beta
**Decided by:** Chey (via Claude)

**Decision:** Voice is a capability of the continuous scanner itself (`apps/web/src/scan/voice/`). The reader talks while scanning and the list corrects itself, so detection only has to be a good default. The smallest version that is safe to put on a real phone:

- **Grammar (closed, fuzzy).** Printing: normal / regular / non-holo, holo / holofoil, reverse holo, plus the modifiers that tell printings of one finish apart (first edition, shadowless, unlimited, Poké Ball, Master Ball, stamped, league, cosmos). Quantity: "two of those", "three copies", "times four", "make it two", a bare "two", "twenty two", and "two reverse holos" (quantity and printing in one breath); number homophones ("to", "for") count only inside a quantity frame. Remove: "remove / delete / discard / get rid of it". Undo: "undo / cancel / never mind / keep it". "Stop listening". Matching is a phonetic-ish edit distance against this list only, because recognizers mishear card words ("reverse hollow" was measured on iOS 18.6) and we cannot teach them. The grammar, fillers and card names must explain every word before anything acts, so conversation that merely contains "reverse holo" does nothing. A stray word is as likely a card name we couldn't find as it is noise, and guessing sends the change to a card nobody meant. The cost is repeating a command that was wrapped in stray words, which is the cheap direction to be wrong in. Anything said with "not", "can't", "don't" or "never" in it counts as an objection, and nothing happens. The exception is "not a holo", which collectors mean as Normal. "No, remove it" still removes, because "no" starts a correction. Nothing happens either for a question or a wish ("is this a reverse holo?", "can I remove the Charizard?", "I might remove it", "do you have a reverse holo?"), for an utterance that names two different cards, for "remove" combined with an edit in one breath, or for a count outside 1–99. A refusal in any of the recognizer's alternative guesses beats a command in another. The last printing said wins, and a printing phrase beats a card that happens to be named like one (the "Poké Ball" trainer card).
- **Targeting.** "That one" is the most recent CAPTURE at the moment the reader starts speaking, pinned on the utterance's first interim words. It is not the most recent row: a card is usually still being identified when the reader talks about it, and iOS finalises about 3 s after speech ends, by which time the next card may have landed. The row id is the capture id, so a command for a card still in the air waits for its row, and is dropped with a message if the capture is discarded. A spoken card name wins over "that one" and means the most recently scanned card of that name, by shutter time rather than by when its row landed. Names of three letters or more count ("Mew"). A word where a name belongs ("the Pikachu is…") that matches no row refuses the command with "Couldn’t find “pikachu”" rather than falling back to "that one".
- **Pending chips, confirm and undo.** A command never mutates the list directly. It becomes a pending chip on its row (with a mic, the printing name as the catalog spells it, a draining bar, and ×). "Remove it" strikes the row through, with Keep. Edits apply after 4 s and removals after 5 s unless tapped away or cancelled with "undo". After that, a spoken "undo" reverts the latest applied change (up to 10 steps), while the Undo on a caption reverts exactly the change that caption describes. A removed row returns to its place in scan order. If the reader picks a printing or steps the count by hand during the hold, their edit wins and the voice change is refused ("Kept the change you made to …"). An undo that would put a printing back onto a card the row has since been corrected into is refused, because that variant belongs to the old card. A spoken printing sets `printingPicked`, exactly as the row's own select does. It is checked again against the row when the hold ends, so it is never applied to a card the row has since been corrected into. Leaving the scan step applies everything pending, since the reader has seen those chips and not objected.
- **While listening.** The Voice toggle sits in the capture bar beside Capture. When the list is expanded, the microphone keeps running while the camera is hidden, so the toggle moves to the list header as an icon and the caption floats above the Verify bar. A caption over the camera shows the words as they stream, then the result ("Venonat → Reverse Holofoil · Undo"), a refusal, or the unmatched words dimmed. A polite `aria-live` region announces outcomes and never interim words. The scanner makes no sound, because any audio playback silently kills the iOS recognizer. Feedback is visual, plus a vibration where the platform supports one (not iOS Safari).
- **Permission flow.** The first tap on a device shows an explainer sheet before any browser prompt, then iOS shows its own speech-recognition prompt and the site microphone prompt. A refusal reads "Blocked" with instructions. A refusal after the microphone has already worked reads "Paused, tap to resume": iOS may want a fresh gesture after the page has been away.
- **Failure states.** The recognizer adapter always requests continuous results and re-arms whenever a session ends, which covers Chrome's session-per-utterance and `no-speech` timeouts. A watchdog replaces a session with no sign of life for 20 s, because iOS can die with no error and no `end`. Four sessions in a row that end within 1 s having heard nothing stop the adapter with the reason ("needs a network connection", "keeps stopping") instead of looping. Hiding the page stops listening, and showing it again resumes. Unmounting the route aborts the session. Where the API is absent (Firefox), the feature is hidden.
- **Privacy copy (the narrow true claim).** "Your browser does the listening. Safari sends your speech to Apple, and Chrome to Google, to turn it into text. DeckPal never receives, records or stores your audio or what you said." No transcript is logged, sent or kept by our code.
- **Opt-in.** A new feature-catalog row, `scanner_voice` ("Scanner voice commands"), at lifecycle **beta** (migration 073). Beta is off for every tier until the account opts in from Profile → Feature preferences, and that includes Owner. It is not **experimental**, because experimental is switched on automatically for Superadmin/Owner. The web reads `features[].enabled`, and no permission is minted, because voice has no server surface.

**Why:** Chey's framing (2026-09-23) is that voice makes an imperfect detector good enough, provided a misheard word can never silently change the collection. A closed grammar with coverage gating, pending-then-apply and undo are the mechanisms that make that true. The catalog gives a server-side kill switch (`disabled`) and a release path (`released`) without a deploy. Real-iPhone transcription beside the live camera is still unverified, so it ships off by default.

**Implications:** Migration 073 must be applied (`pnpm migrate`) before the opt-in appears. Until then the feature is simply absent, which fails closed. It is numbered after #204's 072, and this PR merges after that one. A regular User can see and opt into the beta row but cannot reach the scanner without `scanner.use`, so the opt-in does nothing for them. If that proves confusing, the row can gain a prerequisite later. Still unverified: real transcription on an iPhone, and recognition running alongside the camera's `getUserMedia` (the camera requests `audio: false`). The fixture-server and Chromium checks drive a fake recognizer. Tests: `scan/voice/__tests__` (grammar against real mistranscriptions, printing choice against real catalog slugs, the queue and undo, the adapter on a fake clock) and `tests/browser/scannerVoice.mts` (the shipping hook and controls on Playwright's fake clock, at 1280 and 390 px).
