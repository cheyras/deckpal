## 2026-10-04 — The labeler queue listing reads one snapshot instead of locking every photo
**Decided by:** Chey (via Claude Opus 5.5)

**Decision:** `GET /dev/scan-queue` builds the queue from ONE object listing of `dev-queue/` (both buckets). For each family that listing saw with a photo, the snapshot picks what to show: the replacement JPEG if it was listed, else the original, with the size the listing reported. Only that photo's sidecar is then read (else the original's), with no lock, at most 16 at a time. A family the listing saw only as sidecars is still rechecked at its photo paths under the family lock, two at a time, as before. Reads, repair, cleanup, discard, enqueue and the capture migration keep the family lock unchanged. The response shape and its oldest-first order are unchanged. `QueueStore` gains `list()`, so the listing and its 502-on-outage mapping live in the queue layer instead of the route.

**Why:** Measured 2026-10-05 UTC, the route answered 504 FUNCTION_INVOCATION_TIMEOUT on every call, and nothing (labels or scanner telemetry) had reached `/dev/scan-flags` since 2026-09-27 22:48 UTC: the owner could not work the queue at all. The 2026-09-26 design took the family lock for every listed photo, two at a time, and inside it made up to two Storage HEADs and one or two sidecar reads. The private bucket (2026-09-28) made each absent-object probe three requests, because the read-through checks private, then legacy, then private again. That came to about 0.3–0.8 s per photo, so a few hundred photos passed Vercel's 60 s limit. Now a typical photo costs one sidecar read and no database round trip: 500 photos are one listing plus 500 reads, about 32 waves at 16 in flight. Locking an unlocked read buys nothing. The listing only ever answered "what was here when I looked", and by the time the reader sees it that answer can be stale with or without the lock. What the lock protects is what happens when a photo is opened or changed, and those paths keep it.

**Implications:** The 2026-09-26 guarantees still hold. A photo the snapshot saw is never hidden, because nothing drops a family with a listed photo. A sidecar alone is still not a photo. Each family appears once, under its original ID. A photo the listing missed is still found by the locked recheck, as long as its sidecar was listed. The one thing that moved: a listing that runs while a discard is in progress can now show the discarded photo once, because it no longer waits behind the lock. Opening that entry is a locked read that answers 404, repair has no source to bring it back, and the next listing drops it. Same-device read-your-writes holds: the labeler refreshes only after its delete returns, and the Storage listing is a database query. Sizes come from listing metadata, and only the queue's byte total uses them. A stale entry can show the original's size or metadata until the next refresh, and its ID always opens the current photo. Tests now pin call counts (no lock, no photo probe for a listed family; one listing plus one sidecar read per photo across 500), peak concurrency (16 sidecar reads, 2 locked rechecks) and the stale-entry read. The state table in `apps/api/src/dev/queue-state.md` describes the new listing.
