## 2026-10-10 — Battle logs keep in-person games, countable archetypes and a saved review; Deck-E reads a game as a digest, and Quick asks Sonnet one question
**Decided by:** @cheyras (in-person debrief, judgement sized to the game, "Haiku prompting into Sonnet internally", the close-game rule); built by Claude Opus 5.5.

**Decision:** Migration 084 gives `battle_log` an `origin` (`ptcgl`, `in_person` or `other`; `raw_log` is now nullable, and a `ptcgl` row must still have one), an `opponent_archetype` (a countable key — lowercase ASCII words joined by hyphens, at most 64 characters — that the API normalizes from any label, `N's Zoroark ex` → `ns-zoroark-ex`; old rows are backfilled from simple `opponent_deck` labels and the rest stay NULL) and a `review_md` (at most 12,000 characters). `add_battle_log` and `edit_battle_log` take `origin`, `opponent_archetype` and `review`; an in-person or other game needs a `deck_id` and an explicit `result` and carries no log. `battle_logs` opens with the deck's record per archetype, so "new matchup" and "met three times" are counts. The reader's words go in `notes`, the assistant's analysis in `review`. An in-person game is debriefed before it is logged: Deck-E asks on one `ask_user` card — first for the deck (or the `/decks/<id>` page they are on) and the result when either is missing, then the opponent's main attacker, who went first, the prize race and what decided it, each with ready options — and logs once per game; MCP clients get the same playbook from the server instructions and the `log-battle` prompt. Each game gets one depth, checked in order: **Light** on the game's shape (no Basic, dead draws, an early concession) whatever the opponent; **Deep** for an archetype new to the deck, one met at least three times, a second straight loss into one deck, or a requested breakdown; **Standard** for every other real game. A new read-only tool, `battle_digest` (`GET /decks/:id/logs/:logId/digest`), re-reads a stored Live log into who went first, mulligans, each side's first attack, the prize race turn by turn with the Knock Out each prize paid for, every opposing card seen, how it ended and whether it was close, in under 3,000 characters. Close means the lead changed hands, or a final prize gap of two or less once someone has taken at least three prizes. On the Quick tier only, Deck-E holds `consult`: one question and a brief he writes (≤ 500 and ≤ 8,000 characters) to Claude Sonnet 5.5, at most twice per request, metered on the request; the answer comes back to him, not the reader, and is never grounding evidence. A Standard game is logged, digested, consulted once and its review saved with `edit_battle_log` on its own card; a Deep game does the same with the archetype's record and the earlier games' notes and reviews in the brief; without `consult`, Deck-E writes the review himself. A declined pasted game is remembered by the game (the paste's SHA-256 replaces `@pasted` in the decline key), and a message holding several games logs the last one and says so on the card and in the tool result (`pastedLogCount`). The shared tool descriptions no longer name Deck-E-only things (`showDeck`, "Deck-E's analysis").

**Why:** The owner, 2026-10-10: "even with just Claude, he will log the battle but do the bare minimum — he won't analyse the battle much, and won't write a great summary of the match"; and a paper game had nowhere to go, since `add_battle_log` required a Live log. Matchup frequency was a model's guess over free text ("Dragapult", "Dragapult ex / Dusknoir", the parser's own label), so "met at least three times" could not be checked; a normalized key makes it a `GROUP BY`. The analysis lived only in the chat, mixed into the reader's notes when it was saved at all; `review_md` keeps it, separate, where `battle_review` and `deck_iterate` read it later. For one game a model had two choices — the thin parse or up to 50,000 characters of raw log, most of it "drew a card" — and neither says *when* anything happened, which is what a review is about; the digest is recomputed from the immutable log on every call, so every past game improves when it does, with no backfill. The consult is the owner's "Haiku prompting into Sonnet internally": Haiku 5.5 ties Sonnet 5.5 on bounded tool work and trails it on judgement (2026-10-10 Haiku-first decision), and raising the whole request to Standard would re-bill the conversation on Sonnet for every step of the logging flow, approval cards included, to buy one paragraph. Quick only, because on Standard or Deep a consult asks the same model or a weaker one; two per request is one question plus one rephrasing; never grounding, because every card id in the answer came from Deck-E's own brief, and observing it would let an id he invented launder itself into a panel. The close-game rule is the owner's, with one guard: a turn-two 0–0 concession has a gap of zero and is the opposite of close. Deep Think is not wired (39c5ab9f), so a Deep game gets its fuller treatment on the same turn instead of an offer nothing can honour. The per-game decline key closes the gap #291 left open: a reader who declined one paste's card had every later paste for that deck refused as "already declined". A two-game paste was silently halved. The probe results are under Implications.

**Implications:**
- **Apply migration 084 to production before merging.** The API reads and writes `origin`, `opponent_archetype` and `review_md` on every battle-log route, so code deployed ahead of the schema fails every battle-log read and write. Apply it with the repo's migration runner (B4), not the Supabase CLI.
- `battle_digest` is a read (`readOnlyHint: true`): read-only MCP connections get it, and the ordinary catalogue is now 26 tools, 15 of them reads. It sits with the deck-intelligence tools, so every later tool moved down one place in `tools/list` on the deploy that adds it.
- `consult` is in `buildTools` but removed by `toolsForTier` on Standard and Deep, decided once per request from the tier code chose, so the tool list (and Anthropic's cached tools prefix) never changes between steps. The textless-refusal retry on Standard keeps the Quick set; it is rare, and a constant set matters more. A turn that consulted and then said nothing is still an empty answer (`SERVER_SET` excludes `consult`).
- The consult's system line asks for ≤ 400 words grounded in the brief; its output is capped at 8,000 tokens including adaptive thinking (about eight cents of Sonnet output in the worst case). A failure, abort or empty answer returns "NOT RUN" and Deck-E writes the review himself without claiming anyone else reviewed it.
- Pathway texts (`battle_log`, `battle_review`) were rewritten from the 2026-10-10 pathway audit: a deck question or the deck page for in-person games, `search_cards` for a named attack instead of research before the card, `played_at` when the reader says when, one call per round at an event, never a guessed `player_name`, a three-line review template with four defined causes (variance, misplay, list, matchup), the one-depth rule, full notes through `battle_logs` `log_id` (list rows cut notes to 40 characters), merged archetype keys, and asking what felt wrong when logs are thin. They deliberately say nothing about `dry_run`: tool results already say how to apply, and a "preview first" line once took write calls to 0 of 15. `pathways.test.ts` pins that no pathway mentions Deep Think.
- `packages/agent-tools/src/__tests__/descriptions.test.ts` walks every shared tool's description and argument descriptions for Deck-E-only names.
- Measured on the routed replay probe (n = 2, $0.29 in all; fixture tools now answer `add_battle_log`, `battle_logs`, `battle_digest` and `edit_battle_log` from the scenario's real paste through the real parser and digest, and `consult` with a real Sonnet call): a lopsided paste 2/2 on Quick ($0.005; notes always written, a short reply); a close loss asking "what went wrong?" 2/2, routed to Standard because triage reads that as battle_review ($0.070); an in-person locals game 2/2 ($0.024: the deck read from `decks`, `search_cards` `text` for the named attack, a three- or four-question card with options, then one in-person log with notes and review); the Slowking review 0/2 on a grader that counts no changes in markdown-bold replies (fixed on another branch), with both runs reading the record, `deck_history`, full notes by `log_id` and digests of two or three losses. No live run chose `consult`: the close game went to Standard, and on Quick Haiku judged a 1–3 concession Light. A genuinely close Quick scenario (`battle-log-digest-consult`, 4–6 with the lead changing hands) is in the fixtures but could not run — the Gateway returned 402 for an exhausted credit balance — so `consult` is verified by unit tests, source pins and the fixture path, not yet by a live model choosing it.
- Follow-ups: Deep Think (consent card, larger hold, migration 083) can bring back a Deep-game offer when it ships; a consult for `battle_review` is not needed while that pathway starts on Standard.
