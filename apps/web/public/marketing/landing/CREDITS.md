# Landing photographs: provenance

Every photograph in this directory except `github.*` and the hero film (`hero.*`, below) is a
**generated scene with real card art composited in**. None of them is a photograph of a real person, place or event.

- **Scenes:** Gemini 3 Pro Image (`google/gemini-3-pro-image`) through the Vercel AI
  Gateway, generated by `scripts/gen-landing-photos.mjs` (each shot's prompt is in its
  `SHOTS` table). Every readable card was generated as a blank white face; the prompts
  forbid text, logos and printed card designs.
  - **Round 1, 2026-10-03:** eight "league night" game-store shots, 35 images generated
    (32 Gemini, 2 `openai/gpt-image-2`, 1 `bfl/flux-2-max`). Only `closing` still ships;
    round 2 replaced six others and the hero film (2026-11-28) replaced `hero`.
  - **Round 2, 2026-10-04:** five panel backdrops, each in a different place, light and
    camera angle: `plan` (top-down flat lay of a binder on a desk in daylight, 4:3),
    `playtest` (a home desk at night under a lamp, 3/4 overhead, 4:3), `tune` (a deck laid
    out on a playmat by a window, high angle, 4:3), `build` (singles in toploaders spilling
    from a mailer on a kitchen counter, 4:3) and `ask` (a first-person hand of seven on
    the couch in lamp light, 3:2). 15 Gemini images were generated to reach five picks.
- **Cards:** real card art from DeckPal's own image store
  (`https://deckpal.app/deckpal/images/en/<serie>/<set>/<localId>/high.webp`), used
  nominatively like everywhere else in the app (see the site footer). Composited by
  `tools/landing-photos/composite.py`, which uses each blank face as its light map so the
  art takes the scene's perspective, shading, colour, focus and grain. No card text on the
  page is model-generated. The round-2 cards come from the sample deck in
  `apps/web/src/routes/landing/data.ts` (the `plan` binder holds Pitch Black 028-045 in
  collector order; `build` holds the sample deck's missing singles).
- **Retouching:** generated pseudo-text marks in round 1 were painted out by the same tool.
  Round 2 needed none.
- **`github.*`:** a real screenshot of the public repository page
  (`github.com/cheyras/deckpal`), cropped to the repo name, tabs, commit count and two
  folders, with the notification button masked out. Not generated.
- **Reproducing:** `tools/landing-photos/specs/<shot>.json` freezes every card's position;
  `node tools/landing-photos/fetch-art.mjs` downloads the art they name, `composite.py
  compose specs/<shot>.json` rebuilds a final, and `node scripts/gen-landing-photos.mjs
  encode [--only a,b]` writes the AVIF/WebP widths here plus `MANIFEST.json` (measured
  from disk). Raw candidates and finals live in the gitignored `.marketing-raw/landing/`.

## The hero film (`hero.*`, `hero-square.*`, and the `hero-*` and `hero-square-*` posters)

A 28 s silent, anime-style film (v2, the polish pass of 2026-11-28). It was made outside this
repository, in the owner's separate `deckpal-marketing` workspace, where every prompt, take,
licence, build script and its slop log are kept.

- **Key frames:** original characters and scenes drawn with the Codex CLI's image tool (the
  owner's ChatGPT account). The player and his opponent are original; Deck-E is DeckPal's own
  mascot; no character or logo from any anime or other work was generated. Pokémon appear only
  as card art.
- **Motion:** mostly 2D camera work on those key frames (punch-ins, snap zooms, shakes, whips).
  Veo 3.1 Lite (`google/veo-3.1-lite-generate-001`, through the Vercel AI Gateway, image-to-video,
  1080p, silent) is used only where its frames passed a frame-by-frame audit: the slam's impact,
  the store door, the card spin and the logo burst.
- **Deck-E** is never drawn by a model: he is rendered by DeckPal's own character runtime
  (`src/character/decke`, the shipped rig and playbook), captured frame by frame and cel-shaded.
- **What the model never drew:** every phone and laptop screen is a real DeckPal screen (fresh
  signed-out captures of deckpal.app and DeckPal's own browser-test captures, with real card art
  in the tiles), keyed onto a green screen in 2D and animated as the app behaves (taps, the deck
  widget, the board, the battle log), with a 2D thumb whose taps land as the screen responds. The card layout on the store table is set
  flat to the Pokémon TCG play area and warped into place. All type, the DeckPal logo and the
  effects (speed lines, impact frames, sparkles) are composited.
- **Cards:** real card art from DeckPal's own image store, as above, simplified toward the
  film's cel style. Some cards in the end-card burst (Pikachu, Charizard, Gyarados, Snorlax,
  Mewtwo, Eevee, Venusaur) were drawn by the image model in that style.
- **Encoding:** 1920x1080 at 24 fps; VP9 (`hero.webm`, listed first) and H.264 High with
  `+faststart` (`hero.mp4`), no audio track. The poster is the film's first frame, lightly
  denoised, encoded by `node scripts/gen-landing-photos.mjs encode --only hero`.
- **Phone cut:** `hero-square.webm` and `hero-square.mp4` are the same film at 1080x1080, reframed
  shot by shot from the same renders, with the same timing, encoding and absence of audio. Phones
  below 640px wide get this cut instead of the 16:9 one. Its poster `hero-square-*` (1080/720/480)
  is its own first frame, encoded with `--only hero-square`.
